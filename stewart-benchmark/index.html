Two-stage Stewart platform: exact loop closure, checked against forward kinematics

A two-stage Stewart platform driven by its 12 leg lengths for 18 s. With every joint satisfied, each platform's pose is pure geometry, so the exact answer is known without running any dynamics. This page scores ManifoldsX and a MuJoCo model of the same mechanism against that answer, and gives you everything needed to check it.

Method

How it was measured

Model
Two stacked Stewart platforms: 26 moving bodies, 24 spherical and 12 prismatic joints. The 12 leg lengths follow tables (spline-smoothed, sampled every 1 ms, linear between samples) for 18 s. Both solvers read the same model file.
ManifoldsX
Index-3 HHT (α = 0.3) with a full Newton matrix and local error control at 1e-5, steps up to 3 ms. Joints and leg drives are exact constraints.
MuJoCo 3.15
Same bodies, masses, inertias and joint points. Each loop closes with an equality connect. Each leg follows its table through a joint equality coupled to a virtual clock joint, so the constraint sees the target's velocity as well as its position. A plain C loop around mj_step refreshes the targets every step.
Reference
Forward kinematics. Both stages are solved from the 12 leg lengths with Newton's method at every saved time, to 1e-14 m, with no dynamics and no solver involved.
Metrics
Worst case over the run of the top platform's position error against the reference, the largest loop gap, and the largest leg-length error.
Cost
Single-core wall time for the 18 s run. Re-run on a second machine (2-core cloud instance, MuJoCo 3.15.0): ManifoldsX 13.6×, MuJoCo 1 ms 15.7×, 0.25 ms 4.0× real time (medians), with identical errors.

Why the errors differ

ManifoldsX solves joint and drive positions at every step, so its error is set by its Newton tolerance and doesn't grow with the step size. MuJoCo enforces constraints at acceleration level with stabilization. Each constraint trails by roughly acceleration × time constant², and the time constant can't go below twice the step, so its error falls with the square of the step.

Limits

What this result does not show

  • One model, prescribed motion. It measures closed-loop kinematic accuracy under driven motion. It says nothing about contact, force-driven dynamics, or throughput across many parallel environments, which is what MuJoCo is designed for.
  • MuJoCo has no built-in time-driven joint constraint. The clock-joint drive is my construction from standard MuJoCo features. If you know a better MuJoCo setup for driven loops, I'd like to hear it and add it.
  • ManifoldsX itself is not public. You can rerun the MuJoCo side and the forward-kinematics reference, and score the saved ManifoldsX trajectories, but you can't run ManifoldsX yourself.
  • One machine for the published speeds. A second machine gave the same errors and speed ratios within about 20%.

Correction

An earlier internal version of this benchmark put ManifoldsX at 0.0004 mm. That figure came from scoring against ManifoldsX's own 0.25 ms run through a cubic spline, which overshoots at the 1 ms kinks of the piecewise-linear leg tables. Scored against forward kinematics, it is 0.00002 mm. The MuJoCo numbers didn't change.
