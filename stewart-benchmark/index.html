Benchmark, updated 23 October 2026

Two-stage Stewart platform, checked against forward kinematics

The platform is driven by its 12 leg lengths for 18 seconds. With every joint closed, each platform's pose is pure geometry, so the exact answer is known without running any dynamics. ManifoldsX and a MuJoCo model of the same mechanism are scored against it.

Result

Worst top-platform error over the 18-second run, on one core: ManifoldsX 0.0000008 mm at 13–14× real time. MuJoCo, with the stiffest constraint settings it accepts, 0.11 mm at 1 ms steps (16.6×) and 0.0031 mm at 0.25 ms steps (4×). With MuJoCo's default settings the error is 43 mm at any step size.

One model and one load case. Read the limits below before drawing conclusions.

How it was measured

Model
Two stacked Stewart platforms: 26 moving bodies, 24 spherical and 12 prismatic joints. The 12 leg lengths follow tables (spline-smoothed, sampled every 1 ms, linear between samples) for 18 s. Both solvers read the same model file.
ManifoldsX
Index-3 HHT (α = 0.3) with a full Newton matrix and local error control at 1e-5, steps up to 3 ms. Joints and leg drives are exact constraints.
MuJoCo 3.15
Same bodies, masses, inertias and joint points. Each loop closes with an equality connect. Each leg follows its table through a joint equality coupled to a virtual clock joint, so the constraint sees the target's velocity as well as its position. A plain C loop around mj_step refreshes the targets every step.
Reference
Forward kinematics. Both stages are solved from the 12 leg lengths with Newton's method at every saved time, to 1e-14 m, with no dynamics and no solver involved.
Metrics
Worst case over the run of the top platform's position error against the reference, the largest loop gap, and the largest leg-length error.
Cost
Single-core wall time for the 18 s run. Re-run on a second machine (2-core cloud instance, MuJoCo 3.15.0) on 23 October 2026: ManifoldsX 12.8×, MuJoCo 1 ms 16.4×, 0.25 ms 4.0× real time (medians).

Why the errors differ

ManifoldsX solves joint and drive positions at every step, so its error is set by its Newton tolerance and does not grow with the step size. MuJoCo enforces constraints at acceleration level with stabilization. Each constraint trails by roughly acceleration × time constant², and the time constant cannot go below twice the step, so its error falls with the square of the step.

What this result does not show

  • One model, prescribed motion. It measures closed-loop kinematic accuracy under driven motion. It says nothing about contact, force-driven dynamics, or throughput across many parallel environments, which is what MuJoCo is designed for.
  • MuJoCo has no built-in time-driven joint constraint. The clock-joint drive is built from standard MuJoCo features. A better MuJoCo setup for driven loops is welcome and will be added.
  • ManifoldsX itself is not public. The models, the MuJoCo scripts, the forward-kinematics reference and the saved trajectories are available on request.
  • One machine for the published speeds. A second machine gave the same errors and speed ratios within about 20%.

Correction

An earlier internal version of this benchmark put ManifoldsX at 0.0004 mm. That figure came from scoring against ManifoldsX's own 0.25 ms run through a cubic spline, which overshoots at the 1 ms kinks of the piecewise-linear leg tables. Scored against forward kinematics, it was 0.00002 mm. The MuJoCo numbers did not change.

Update, 23 October 2026

ManifoldsX now accepts a step only when every constraint meets its tolerance; before, only the average over all constraints had to. Same model and settings, re-run: the error-controlled run went from 0.00002 to 0.0000008 mm and the fixed 1 ms run from 0.0097 to 0.0012 mm, at the same speed. The MuJoCo numbers did not change.
