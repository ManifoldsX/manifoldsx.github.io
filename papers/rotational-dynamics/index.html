Three studies in rotational dynamics

A gimballed gyroscope, a spinning top and a tumbling body. Three familiar motions, checked against mechanics.

Read the five-page reportWatch the simulations ↓

We simulated each case for 12 seconds at fixed steps of 1, 0.5 and 0.25 ms. All nine runs completed without retries or cutbacks. The animations below use the motion calculated by ManifoldsX at 1 ms.

01 / Three degrees of freedom

Gimballed gyroscope

The rotor sits inside two freely moving gimbals. We start it spinning and give the whole assembly an initial rotation about the outer hinge. The spinning rotor couples the motion of the two gimbals.

What we checked

Energy, angular momentum along the outer hinge axis, and rotor axial spin should stay constant. Halving the time step changes the orientation of any body by at most 8.42 × 10⁻¹¹ rad.

02 / Steady precession

Spinning top

The top spins about its own axis while that axis travels around the vertical. We choose the initial motion for steady precession, so its tilt remains constant.

What we checked

The full orientation is compared with the exact steady-precession solution. At a 1 ms step, the maximum error is 3.68 × 10⁻⁸ rad. Halving the step reduces that error by about 31 times.

03 / Intermediate-axis instability

Tumbling body

A free body starts spinning almost entirely about its intermediate inertia axis. A small disturbance grows until the body flips. The motion repeats without an external torque.

What we checked

The simulation captures four reversals in 12 seconds. Its angular velocity agrees with the analytical Euler-top solution to within 1.57 × 10⁻⁹ rad/s at the sampled comparison times.

All runs use HEDOP5 with position and velocity projection. The top is checked every step; the tumbling solution every 5 ms. The report gives the full settings, parameters and reference methods.
