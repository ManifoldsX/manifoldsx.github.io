Technical report, 20 October 2026

Three studies in rotational dynamics

A gimballed gyroscope, a spinning top and a tumbling body: three familiar motions, checked against mechanics.

Each case was simulated for 12 seconds at fixed steps of 1, 0.5 and 0.25 ms. All nine runs completed without retries or step cuts. The animations use the 1 ms runs.

Report (PDF, 5 pages)

Gimballed gyroscope

The rotor sits inside two freely moving gimbals. It starts spinning while the whole assembly is given an initial rotation about the outer hinge, and the spinning rotor couples the motion of the two gimbals.

What was checked

Energy, angular momentum about the outer hinge and the rotor's axial spin should stay constant. Halving the time step changes the orientation of any body by at most 8.4×10−11 rad.

Spinning top

The top spins about its own axis while that axis travels around the vertical. The initial motion is set for steady precession, so the tilt stays constant.

What was checked

The full orientation is compared with the exact steady-precession solution. At a 1 ms step the largest error is 3.7×10−8 rad; halving the step reduces it about 31 times.

Tumbling body

A free body starts spinning almost exactly about its intermediate axis of inertia. A small disturbance grows until the body flips, and the flip repeats without any external torque.

What was checked

The simulation captures four reversals in 12 seconds. Its angular velocity agrees with the analytical Euler-top solution to within 1.6×10−9 rad/s at the sampled times.

The top shows the expected fifth-order fall in error. The tumbling body stays within a few billionths of a radian per second; smaller steps do not consistently improve it.

All runs use HEDOP5 with position and velocity projection. The top is checked every step and the tumbling body every 5 ms. The report gives the full settings, parameters and reference methods.
